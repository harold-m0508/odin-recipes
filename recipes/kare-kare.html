Kare-Kare

Description

Kare-Kare is a beloved Filipino stew featuring fall-off-the-bone oxtail and hearty vegetables simmered in a thick, creamy peanut sauce. This deeply satisfying comfort dish is packed with bold, savory flavor — perfect for a cozy family meal or as the star of any special gathering!

Engredients

  • Oxtail: Prized for its rich, flavorful meat and naturally gelatinous quality, oxtail becomes melt-in-your-mouth tender when slow-cooked low and slow.
  • Vegetables: This recipe calls for banana heart, eggplant, long beans, and pechay. Feel free to also throw in bok choy, cabbage, or Baguio beans for extra variety.
  • Rice flour: A convenient and accessible substitute for whole rice grains. Toast it in a dry skillet first to unlock its nutty aroma and deepen the flavor. Avoid glutinous rice flour as it won't work the same way. No rice flour on hand? Simply toast raw rice in a dry pan until golden, then blitz it in a blender or grind it with a mortar and pestle.
  • Peanut butter: The heart of the sauce — it brings a creamy, nutty richness that gives Kare-Kare its signature depth and body. Use a good-quality peanut butter for the best results.
  • Annatto powder: Responsible for the stew's warm yellow-orange hue. If working with annatto seeds instead, soak them in water for a few minutes or heat them in oil to draw out the color. Use the tinted water in the stew or the infused oil for sautéing.
  • Fish sauce: Adds a savory, salty punch and a layer of umami that ties the whole dish together.
  • Aromatics: A base of onion and garlic builds a flavorful foundation that gives the stew its aromatic depth.
  • Salt and pepper: The finishing touch — season to taste to bring all the flavors into balance and add a gentle kick.

Steps

  1. Cook oxtail. Rinse the oxtail under cold running water and trim away any excess fat. Place in a large pot and cover with cold water. Bring to a boil, skimming off any scum that rises to the surface. Reduce the heat, cover, and let it simmer — adding more water as needed — until the oxtail is fork-tender. Drain and set the broth aside.
  2. Prepare the banana heart. Peel back the tough, fibrous outer layers until you reach the lighter, tender core. Trim the stem and slice the core into rounds. Soak the cut pieces in a bowl of cold salted water, then squeeze firmly with your hands to draw out the bitter sap. Rinse thoroughly under cold water, then blanch in a pot of boiling water. Remove with a slotted spoon and set aside.
  3. Blanch the vegetables in the same pot of boiling water, cooking one type at a time. Remove each batch with a slotted spoon and set aside.
  4. Toast the rice flour in a dry skillet over medium heat, stirring frequently, until lightly golden and fragrant.
  5. Combine the toasted rice flour, annatto powder, and 1 cup of the reserved broth in a bowl. Whisk together until completely smooth and lump-free.
  6. Brown the oxtail. Heat oil in a large pot over medium heat and sauté the onions and garlic until softened and fragrant. Add the oxtail and cook until lightly browned on all sides. Stir in the fish sauce.
  7. Add the remaining reserved broth and bring to a boil. Pour in the rice flour mixture, whisking constantly to prevent any lumps from forming.
  8. Stir in the peanut butter and mix until evenly incorporated. Season with salt and pepper to taste, then simmer for 7 to 10 minutes until the sauce has slightly thickened.
  9. Add the blanched vegetables to the kare-kare and cook just until heated through and tender, yet still with a slight crispness.
